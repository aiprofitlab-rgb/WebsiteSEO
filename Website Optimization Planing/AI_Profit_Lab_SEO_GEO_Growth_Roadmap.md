### Move one — deal with Google Analytics 

188 KB transferred to record page views is a poor trade, and it is the largest single cost on your site. Three options, in order of preference: 

- Replace it. A privacy-first analytics script (Plausible, Fathom, Umami) is roughly 1–3 KB — a 98% reduction on your heaviest asset. You lose Google Ads remarketing audiences; if you are not running Google Ads, you lose nothing you use. 

- Defer it. Keep GA4 but load it after first interaction or on `requestIdleCallback` . Costs you a small number of bounced sessions in the data; costs the user nothing during paint. 

Server-side tag it. Most robust, most work. Only worth it if you commit to Google Ads later. 

Given you are one operator optimising for organic and LLM visibility rather than paid media, replace it. Keep GA4 running in parallel for one month if you want continuity in the numbers, then drop it. 

### Move two — externalise the money-page CSS 

Your blog already does this correctly and your money pages do not. The blog pattern — one content-hashed 

stylesheet, immutably cached, shared across every page — means a reader who opens three articles downloads the CSS once. On the money pages, a visitor going homepage → services → checkout downloads 51 KB, then 38 KB, then 39 KB of largely identical CSS. Applying the blog's own pattern in `tools/build_v4.py` fixes it. 

Keep a small critical-CSS block inline — roughly the first 8–14 KB covering the header and hero — and move the rest out. That preserves fast first paint while making every subsequent page nearly free.

## `06` GEO / AEO — becoming the answer 

You chose LLM citation over classic ranking as a priority. That is the right call for your market, and you are closer to it than you probably think — but the last mile is not about content. 

### What you have already won 

Most sites fail LLM citation at the first hurdle: the crawler is blocked, or there is nothing machine-readable to consume. You have cleared both. Your `robots.txt` explicitly admits every major AI crawler, and your `llms.txt` gives them a single, clean, factual document containing your services, your prices in OMR, your legal entity, your founder, your contact details and your URL map. It even pre-empts the brand collision with the unrelated `aiprofitlab.com` trading products. That file is doing real work, and it is better than what most GCC agencies have. 

### How LLMs actually decide who to cite 

Three signals, roughly in this order of weight: 

1. Corroboration across independent sources. A model trusts a fact it has seen in several places that are not you. This is the single biggest lever and the one you are weakest on. 

2. Extractability. Can a specific, quotable claim be lifted cleanly from your page and attributed? Direct answers, definitions, numbers, and tables are extractable. Persuasive prose is not. 

3. Entity clarity. Does the model know who you are, where you are, and what you do — unambiguously? This is where your brand collision hurts and where `llms.txt` and schema help. 

###### T H E U N C O M F O R TA B L E PA R T 

Signal one cannot be produced on your own website. No amount of on-site optimisation makes an LLM cite you if nothing off-site corroborates you. Your two existing backlink documents are the right instinct, but treat their DR/traffic figures as unverified — they read as estimates rather than pulled data. The value in those documents is the target list, not the metrics.

## `08` Turning visitors into buyers
