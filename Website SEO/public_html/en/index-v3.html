The questions I actually get asked

Straight answers, including the awkward ones.

I found something cheaper. Why is yours more?
Because those are tools and this is a build. With a subscription tool, somebody in your office has to configure it, keep it current and fix it when it breaks, and you are renting it forever. Here I do all of that, it is one payment, and the site is yours at the end. If nobody in your company wants to own a tool, the cheap option is not actually cheaper. If somebody does, it genuinely is, and I will say so.
Am I locked into a monthly fee?
No. The one-time fee includes a full year of hosting, security and care. After that year the hosting is a small direct cost you can pay yourself, or you can take the Growth Desk at OMR 75 a month if you want me improving it. Nothing stops working if you say no.
Who owns the website?
You do. The domain is registered in your business name, the files are yours, and if you ever want to move to another provider I hand everything over without an argument. This is the difference between buying and renting, and it is the main reason to pay once rather than monthly.
I don’t have good photos or written content. Is that a problem?
No, and it is the normal situation. The 20-question intake plus your existing price list and product catalogue is enough for me to write the first version in both languages. You review and correct it. If you have photos of your products, warehouse or team, they help — but a missing photo has never stopped a build.
My buyers only use WhatsApp. Why do I need a website at all?
Because the buyer checks you before they message you, and increasingly asks Google or ChatGPT who supplies something in Oman before they check anyone at all. The storefront is not there to replace WhatsApp — it feeds it. Every serious buyer it finds is handed straight to your WhatsApp with their question already answered and their details already captured.
How long does it take?
Days rather than months, once your intake comes back. The five-day intake deadline in the guarantee exists because that is the step that decides the timeline — the build itself is fast, waiting on answers is what makes projects slow.
Do I need to understand any of the technology?
No. If a sentence in a proposal needs a glossary, it is a badly written sentence. You will be asked about your products, your prices and your delivery, which you already know better than I do.
What if it just doesn’t work for my business?
Then you get your money back. That is the whole reason the First Inquiry Promise is written around one countable event rather than a feeling. And in the first seven days you can walk away for any reason without explaining yourself. I would rather lose a build than argue with somebody about an invoice they do not think they owe.
