## 1. The Avatar — "The Distributor Owner"

The single person every page, offer, email and ad is written for.

**Who he is:** the owner-operator of a small trading, wholesale or distribution business in Oman
or the wider GCC — importers, wholesalers, and distributors of medical consumables, FMCG,
building materials, auto parts, industrial supply.

| Dimension | Profile |
|---|---|
| **Business size** | ~5–50 staff |
| **Tech setup** | No in-house IT or developer. Runs the business on WhatsApp + spreadsheets + basic accounting software |
| **Role in the business** | Still personally the bottleneck: he answers the quote requests, he chases the follow-ups, he is the one who knows what's happening |
| **Language** | Bilingual reality — buyers write in Arabic, suppliers and datasheets are in English. Both must work |
| **Geography** | Oman first, then GCC (UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain) |
| **Buying authority** | Owner. No procurement committee, no IT sign-off. One conversation closes it |

### Why this avatar
Chosen for **founder-fit, not network-fit.** Nahid has personally built and run a real
distribution business and has felt these exact pains. He does **not** have an Omani business
network — his distribution company operates in Iran, not Oman. See §5.

### What he actually feels (his words, not ours)
- "I saw the message at 11pm and by the time I replied he'd already ordered from someone else."
- "I don't know my cash or stock position without phoning someone."
- "I'm answering the same five questions every day."
- "My website is a brochure. It doesn't do anything."

### Jobs to be done
1. Stop losing buyers to messages answered too late.
2. Look credible to a serious buyer who is checking him out before calling.
3. Know who is about to buy, before they call.
4. Get capacity without hiring, visas, insurance and paid leave.

### Buying trigger
He buys off a **frustration event**, not a referral: a missed inquiry, a buyer who went quiet,
a quote he forgot to chase. That matters commercially — with no warm referral engine, the
offer has to be strong enough to close a cold stranger on its own merits.

### What he is NOT buying
Software. He is buying *the outcome of software he never has to touch*. Any copy that makes
him feel he'll have to learn, configure, or maintain something has already lost him.

## 2. The Anti-Avatar — who we say no to

| Not for us | Why |
|---|---|
| Enterprise / ERP-scale buyers | Different weight class, long procurement, we lose to 4Ys and system integrators |
| Pure price shoppers | We are **not** the cheap option locally (see §4). A price-led buyer chooses QwicLink |
| Businesses with no digital or WhatsApp presence at all | Nothing to automate yet; the wedge has no fulcrum |
| DTC / growth-hacking / "make me go viral" buyers | Wrong problem, wrong vocabulary, wrong success metric |
| Anyone wanting a self-serve DIY SaaS tool | Our model is done-for-you; a DIY buyer will churn and blame the product |

## 3. Secondary persona — the Operations / Office Manager

Not the buyer, but the **veto**. If she thinks the system replaces her, she'll kill it quietly.

- Cares about: fewer repetitive tasks, fewer errors, not being blamed.
- Frame automation as **capacity, not replacement** — this is a strategic requirement in the
  Omani market, not a nicety. Never sell "fire your admin."

## 4. Two constraints that override everything

These are real and must shape every piece of copy:

1. **We are not the local price leader.** QwicLink publishes OMR 15/29/49 per month; their AI
   tier lands around OMR 617 in year one against roughly OMR 1,400 for our Starter — about
   2.3× more expensive. "Affordable for GCC SMEs" holds against *enterprise/global tools* and
   against *hiring*. It never holds against local rivals. Differentiate on **done-for-you
   scope**, not price.
2. **Zero published clients is the binding constraint.** Nuqta publishes six named clients
   including Oman Flour Mills; we publish none. Named-competitor "vs" pages must not go live
   until 2–3 references exist — such a page invites a side-by-side comparison we currently lose.
   This is precisely what the Founding Partner mechanism exists to fix (see the Money Model doc).

## 5. Founder story — the approved version

**Use this:** "An engineer who has built and run a real distribution business and personally
felt these exact problems."

**True, and safe.** It doesn't name a country and doesn't imply local roots.

**Never imply:** Omani business roots, a local trade network, warm referrals, or that the
distribution experience was built in-market. His medical-consumables distribution company
operates in **Iran**, not Oman, and he has said plainly: "I don't have connections here."
Claiming otherwise would be false and is easy to check.

## 6. Voice for this avatar

**Words to use:** buyers, orders, quotes, bulk, stock, cash position, follow-up, inquiry,
one-time, included, plain, real.

**Words to avoid:** synergy, leverage, transform, revolutionary, cutting-edge, disrupt,
seamless, "10x", any Silicon-Valley register, and any unqualified statistic.

**Register:** plain-spoken, anti-hype, founder-voiced, first person where a person is
actually speaking. State what's *not* included as openly as what is — that honesty pattern is
a trust device, not a weakness.

**Never publish these unsourced stats** (they appear in older material and cannot be
substantiated): ~40% capacity increase, ~78% overhead reduction, ~0.01% error rate,
99.9% uptime as a contractual promise. The 78% figure may be used *only* when labelled as
derived from our own published cost comparison.
